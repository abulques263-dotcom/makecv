Weight category, activity and next steps

After you calculate BMI, the result will show a broad category. For adults aged 18 and over, it may also show the mathematical difference between your current weight and a standard BMI screening boundary. That difference is not a recommended amount to lose or gain. BMI cannot decide an individual's healthy target by itself.

General movement guidance: children and teenagers are generally encouraged to average around 60 minutes a day of enjoyable activity across the week; adults are generally encouraged to build toward at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening activity on 2 days. Start at a comfortable level, adapt for disability or health conditions, and take rest when needed. These are broad public-health guidelines, not a personal prescription.

If you are under 18, this page will not recommend a weight-loss amount or target weight. If you're worried about your growth or weight, speak with a trusted adult and qualified healthcare professional. If you're an adult and concerned about your result, a clinician can help interpret it alongside other health factors.

How BMI is calculated

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres × height in metres).

For adults aged 18+, BMI categories are broad screening indicators only. The calculator may show an estimated range based on the adult BMI 18.5–24.9 range; it is not a personal target weight and may not suit everyone.

BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat or assess health on its own. For children and teenagers, interpretation uses age- and sex-specific growth charts rather than adult cut-offs. This calculator does not provide a target weight or weight-gain/weight-loss recommendation for anyone under 18.

Frequently asked questions

Will this tell me exactly how much I should weigh?

No. For adults it can show a broad screening range based on BMI, but it is not a personal target. For under-18s, this page does not estimate a target weight.

Does BMI tell me whether I need to gain weight?

Not by itself. BMI is only one screening measure. A qualified health professional can interpret it alongside growth, medical history, and other factors.

Is the meal planner AI-powered?

No. Meal suggestions are generated from simple on-page rules, and no personal data is sent to an AI service. A real AI integration would need a secure backend and an appropriately protected API key.

Are adult BMI categories suitable for teens?

No. Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific interpretation by an appropriate health professional.
